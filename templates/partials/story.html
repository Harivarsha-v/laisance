I

The fibre

On the high grasslands of Inner Mongolia, the goats grow a winter down finer than any wool. Each spring it is combed out by hand, never shorn, a few grams from each animal.

II

The cloth

In Biella, in the Italian Alps, the mills spin it with mountain water and weave it twice over: two faces of cashmere bound as one, so the coat needs no lining at all.

III

The finish

The nap is raised slowly with dried teasel heads, as it has been for centuries, until the cloth is soft enough that a mill master will put his name to it.

IV

The details

From a family tannery in Tuscany, vegetable-tanned calfskin for the facings, and buttons of real horn, each one polished by hand.

V

The cut

In our Paris atelier the pattern is drawn on kraft paper and the cloth is cut with long steel shears, in a single unhurried line.

VI

The hand

The two faces are parted at every edge, folded in and closed with invisible stitches. A single coat passes through eleven pairs of hands.

VII

Paris

Finished in the house where it was drawn, in numbers small enough to know each one. Then it is yours, to be worn for decades and still feel effortless.
